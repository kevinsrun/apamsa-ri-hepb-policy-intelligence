Core Research Findings

  • Concentration in Providence County: 82.6% of Rhode Island's reported chronic hepatitis B cases and 80.8% of recorded screening services occur in Providence County, correlating directly with foreign-born immigrant residency (20.8% foreign-born).
  • Triple Panel Discordance: While HBsAg was billed 767 times in Medicare Part B data, total anti-HBc was billed 607 times (79.1%) and anti-HBs 593 times (77.3%). Clinicians frequently order partial panels, omitting immunity confirmation or core antibody detection.
  • Severe Coverage Gap in Medicare FFS: Medicare Fee-For-Service primarily represents adults ≥65. The highest-prevalence demographic (working-age foreign-born adults aged 25-54) is substantially underrepresented in Medicare Part B claims.
  • Diagnostic Billing Fragmentation: ~68.4% of outpatient laboratory testing in Rhode Island is billed by independent commercial laboratories (Quest/Labcorp) rather than physician practices, meaning physician-level claims reflect ordering locations, not total lab volume.

Methodological Safeguards

Important Clinical & Analytical Bounds:

• Three billed services never equate to one certified individual triple-panel screening.
• Absence of claims does not imply noncompliance; primary care clinicians routinely utilize external reference labs.
• Zero individual patient health information (PHI) is contained or accessible.

Clinical Panel Implications

Under the CDC 2023 guidelines, ordering HBsAg alone is no longer considered adequate adult screening. If anti-HBs and total anti-HBc are omitted:

  • Missed Susceptibility: Clinicians cannot determine whether an uninfected patient needs the hepatitis B vaccine series.
  • Missed Resolved Infection: Clinicians cannot identify patients with resolved past infection who carry risk of viral reactivation during immunosuppressive therapies (e.g. chemotherapy, biologic agents).
  • Order Set Optimization: Electronic Health Record (EHR) order sets in Lifespan and Care New England must bundle the three tests into a single click "Universal Adult HBV Screen".

Geographic Disparity Insights

Sharp Urban Concentration: Providence County exhibits almost 5x the rate of reported chronic Hepatitis B compared to Washington County. Pawtucket, Central Falls, and Providence city zip codes house Rhode Island's largest diaspora populations from Southeast Asia (Cambodian, Hmong, Vietnamese), East Asia (Chinese), West Africa (Nigerian, Ghanaian, Liberian), and the Dominican Republic/Guatemala.

Community Health Center Role

FQHCs like Providence Community Health Centers (PCHC), Blackstone Valley Community Health Care (BVCHC), and Thundermist operate on the frontline of hepatitis B care, providing culturally and linguistically concordant services regardless of insurance or citizenship status.

Analytical Boundaries & Methodological Limitations

1. Claims Aggregation vs Patient Screening: In administrative claims datasets, CPT codes represent billing events rather than confirmed unique patients receiving all components simultaneously. A count of 767 HBsAg services and 607 anti-HBc services indicates an ordering discrepancy, but individual patient linkage requires de-identified all-payer longitudinal claims.

2. The Medicare Part B FFS Denominator Gap: Rhode Island's high-risk foreign-born population is predominantly working age (ages 25-54) and covered through employer-sponsored insurance, Medicaid, or uninsurance. Extrapolating Medicare Part B screening rates to the statewide adult population introduces substantial selection bias.

3. Reference Laboratory Billing: Outpatient physician offices frequently do not process serology on-site; specimens are routed to commercial reference laboratories which submit claims directly. Provider-level counts undercount the actual ordering volume of community clinicians.
