H O U S E   R E S O L U T I O N  
PROCLAIMING THE MONTH OF MAY, 2005 AS "VIRAL HEPATITIS AWARENESS 
MONTH" 
Introduced By: Representatives McNamara, Davey, Watson, Singleton, and Mumford  
Date Introduced: May 10, 2005  
Referred To: House read and passed  
 
 
WHEREAS, All Viral Hepatitides (A, B, & C) are preventable through education or 1 
immunization; and 2 
WHEREAS, According to the Rhode Island Department of Health, it is estimated that 3 
more than 18,000 Rhode Islanders are infected with the Hepatitis C virus; and 4 
WHEREAS, Of those infected with Hepatitis C, 80 – 85% will develop a chronic 5 
infection and an estimated 10,000 Rhode Islanders will develop chronic liver disease; and 6 
WHEREAS, The regional cost of a liver transplant currently averages about $350,000 for 7 
immediate hospital and doctor expenses indicating that HCV  prevention, testing and treatment  8 
should be considered as cost-effective public policies; and 9 
WHEREAS, Viral Hepatitis is typically asymptomatic, and therefore infected individuals 10 
may unknowingly transmit the virus to others; and 11 
WHEREAS, Persons infected with Hepatitis B or Hepatitis C may not learn of their 12 
infection until 10 to 30 years later when complications arise from chronic viral Hepatitis; and 13 
WHEREAS, Hepatitis C infection is the largest single cause for liver transplantation and 14 
one of the principal causes of liver cancer and cirrhosis; and 15 
WHEREAS, Chronic Hepatitis C infection is the most common indication for liver 16 
transplantation in the United States; and 17 
WHEREAS, There are now more than 20,000 people in the United States waiting for a 18 
liver transplant, but there are currently only about 4,900 livers available each year; and 19

WHEREAS, Vaccinations exist for Hepatitis A and Hepatitis B, and there is a need to 1 
promote immunizations, especially for those who are infected with other Hepatitis viruses; and 2 
WHEREAS, No vaccine exis ts for Hepatitis C, and treatments have limited success, 3 
making this infection among the most costly diseases in terms of health care costs, lost wages, 4 
and reduced productivity; and 5 
WHEREAS, Approximately one-third of HIV infected persons in the United St ates are 6 
also infected with Hepatitis C; and 7 
WHEREAS, Liver failure from Hepatitis C has become the leading cause of death for 8 
people living with HIV; and 9 
WHEREAS, Hepatitis C infection progresses more rapidly to liver damage in HIV  10 
infected persons and may impact the course and management of HIV infection; and 11 
WHEREAS, Therapy for Hepatitis C is evolving, and currently recommended regimens 12 
are effective in approximately 50 percent of patients; and 13 
WHEREAS, Early diagnosis can lead to the possibility of preventing further transmission 14 
of viral Hepatitis; and 15 
WHEREAS, Prevention education and other preventive measures, such as vaccination, 16 
can lead to the prevention of viral hepatitis; and 17 
WHEREAS, Members of the House of Representatives as individuals are i n a unique 18 
position to help raise public awareness about the need for increased funding for prevention 19 
education and immunization for Hepatitis A and B, and increased research on viral Hepatitis; 20 
now, therefore be it 21 
RESOLVED, That the community organizati ons and health care providers should be 22 
applauded for their role in promoting awareness about viral Hepatitis and the importance of early 23 
detection, prevention education, other preventive measures including vaccination and ongoing 24 
screening; and be it further 25 
RESOLVED, That the Federal Government, with respect to viral Hepatitis be urged to 26 
endeavor to raise awareness about the importance of prevention education, other preventive 27 
measures, including vaccination, counseling, and appropriate treatment; increa se funding for 28 
prevention education, other preventive measures, and research, so that transmission of viral 29 
Hepatitis is reduced and early detection of viral Hepatitis is promoted; increase the efforts to find 30 
more effective treatments and cures, and conti nue to consider ways to improve access to high 31 
quality health care services for the early detection and treatment of viral Hepatitis; and be it 32 
further 33 
RESOLVED, That this House of Representatives of the State of Rhode Island and 34
